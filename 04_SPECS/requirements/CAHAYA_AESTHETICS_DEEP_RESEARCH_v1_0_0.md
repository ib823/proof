## PART I: THE FOUNDING — FECHNER'S EXPERIMENTAL AESTHETICS

### 1.1 Gustav Theodor Fechner (1801–1887)

Fechner was a physicist, philosopher, and physiologist who founded both psychophysics AND experimental aesthetics. His "Elements of Psychophysics" (1860) established the relationship between physical properties and conscious experience. His "Vorschule der Aesthetik" (1876) was the first systematic empirical study of beauty.

**Revolutionary Contribution:** "Aesthetics from below" — a bottom-up procedure founded on elementary perceptions (preference) rather than top-down philosophical speculation about beauty. Fechner believed mathematical proportions (specifically the golden section) might directly correspond to pleasingness, fitting his psychophysical framework: physical stimulus properties → sensations.

### 1.2 Fechner's Three Experimental Methods (1871, 1876)

1. **Method of Choice:** Show subjects aesthetic stimuli, evoke preference (e.g., select most pleasing rectangle from a set)
2. **Method of Production:** Subject creates stimulus conforming to their preference (e.g., draw rectangle in preferred proportions, divide line at preferred point)
3. **Method of Use:** Examine pre-existing objects to test aesthetic hypotheses (e.g., analyze proportions in famous paintings)

These three methods remain foundational to experimental aesthetics today.

### 1.3 The Golden Rectangle Experiment

Fechner presented 10 rectangles with height/length ratios from 1.00 (square) to 0.40. The Golden Rectangle had ratio 0.62 (φ = 1.618). Fechner reported that 76% of all choices centered on three rectangles having ratios of 0.57, 0.62, and 0.67, with a peak at the 0.62 "Golden Rectangle."

**Initial Interpretation:** Mathematical beauty confirmed. The golden ratio IS the formula for aesthetic preference.

### 1.4 THE REPLICATION CRISIS — 150 Years of Failure

**Höge (1995) — "Fechner's Experimental Aesthetics and the Golden Section Hypothesis Today":**
- Recreated Fechner's experiments with different verbal criteria
- Found that "minute variations in instruction or experimental arrangements may cause differences in proportions preferred" (Berlyne)
- Concluded: "The nice peak of preference Fechner reported for the golden section seems to be either an artifact or it is an effect of still unknown factors"
- **Verdict: "The golden section hypothesis is a myth"**

**Phillips, Norman & Beers (2010) — "Fechner's Aesthetics Revisited":**
- Recreated experiment using more naturalistic stimuli (not just abstract rectangles)
- Evaluated preferences against various complexity metrics
- **Finding:** Subjects prefer EITHER very simple OR very complex objects
- Result "runs contrary to hypothesized results, but are systematic none the less"
- Conclusion: "There are likely to be useful measures of aesthetic preference but they are likely to be complicated by the difficulty in defining some of their constituent parts"

**McManus, Cook & Hunt (2010) — "Beyond the Golden Section and Normative Aesthetics":**
- Used paired comparison method with 79 participants
- **CRITICAL FINDING:** WEAK population preferences but STRONG, STABLE, STATISTICALLY ROBUST and VERY VARIED INDIVIDUAL preferences
- Individual differences in rectangle preferences are ENORMOUS
- Cultural and contextual factors have significant influence
- **Verdict:** Golden section has "little normative role in rectangle preferences"

**Stone & Collins — Binocular Visual Field Hypothesis:**
- Attempted neurobiological explanation: shape of binocular visual field might determine rectangle preference
- Asked participants to draw pleasing rectangles
- Average rectangle ratio: ~1.5 (NOT golden ratio 1.618)
- Subsequent experiments FAILED to replicate even these results

**Christopher Green — "All that Glitters":**
- Comprehensive historical review of golden ratio experiments
- **Key finding:** "The only aspect of Golden Ratio preference experimentation seems to be the inconsistency of their results"

**Keith Devlin (Mathematician, Stanford):**
- "Let's put it this way, if someone comes along tomorrow with a scientific explanation for why the Golden ratio would play a role in aesthetics and whatever else, then we'd all revise our opinion... But on the science side, there's no evidence."

**Anthony Waichulis (Vision Science Researcher):**
- "Current research in fields like empirical aesthetics, vision science, perceptual neuroscience, etc... is just not supportive of such claims (without significant misrepresentation and misunderstanding)."

### 1.5 Implication for CAHAYA

**CAHAYA must NOT enforce the golden ratio as an aesthetic requirement.** There is no scientific basis for it. However, CAHAYA can enforce acceptable ratio RANGES (approximately 1.3–1.8) as perceptually comfortable proportions, while acknowledging that individual preferences within this range are legitimate.

## PART II: BERLYNE'S AROUSAL THEORY — THE INVERTED-U

### 2.1 Daniel Berlyne (1960–1976)

Berlyne proposed a psychobiological theory of aesthetics: aesthetic preference follows an inverted-U (Wundt curve) relationship with arousal/complexity.

**Core Hypothesis:** Intermediate arousal = maximum pleasure. Too simple = boring. Too complex = overwhelming. Optimal point in middle.

**Collative Variables:** Novelty, complexity, uncertainty, incongruity — all contribute to arousal potential.

### 2.2 Empirical Evidence — MIXED

**Supporting:**
- Marin & Leder (2013, 2016): Strong relationship between complexity and arousal (r > 0.85). When controlling for familiarity, complexity-beauty relationship showed inverted-U. Arousal identified as mediator between complexity and pleasantness.

**Contradicting:**
- Zajonc's mere exposure effect: Monotonic INCREASING relationship (not inverted-U) — repeated exposure increases liking
- Martindale & Moore (1988, 1989): Prototypicality shows monotonic increase in preference
- Althuizen (2021): Product design study with 1800+ participants — "scant evidence for inverted U-shape"
- Chmiel & Schubert (2017): Review of 57 studies over 115 years — inconsistent results across domains

### 2.3 Current Status

"Berlyne's arousal theory of aesthetic appreciation has been mostly abandoned" (Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy). The inverted-U exists in SOME contexts but is not universal. The theory fails because it assumes a single optimal complexity level for all people, when in fact preferences are highly individual.

### 2.4 Implication for CAHAYA

**CAHAYA should NOT enforce a single optimal complexity level.** Instead, enforce complexity BOUNDS (minimum: not empty/trivial; maximum: not chaotic/overwhelming) while allowing individual preference within the range. The inverted-U serves as a heuristic warning, not a hard constraint.

## PART III: PROCESSING FLUENCY THEORY

### 3.1 Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman (2004)

"Processing Fluency and Aesthetic Pleasure: Is Beauty in the Perceiver's Processing Experience?"

**Core Principle:** The more fluently (easily) perceivers can process an object, the more positive their aesthetic response.

**What Increases Fluency:**
- Symmetry (less information to process)
- Figure-ground contrast
- Prototypicality (familiarity)
- Repeated exposure
- Good Gestalt (simplicity, regularity)

### 3.2 Empirical Support — STRONG

- EMG measurements show zygomaticus muscle activity (smiling) correlates with fluency
- Prototype preference experiments confirm fluency effect
- Explains mere exposure effect and symmetry preference
- Cross-modal: works in visual, auditory, and tactile domains

### 3.3 Critical Nuance — Attribution & Expectation

Fluency only affects aesthetic judgment when the source of fluency is UNKNOWN to the perceiver. If a viewer attributes their easy processing to simplicity (rather than beauty), the fluency effect is discounted. This explains why extremely simple designs can feel "boring" rather than "beautiful" — the viewer attributes fluency to triviality.

### 3.4 Implication for CAHAYA

**Processing fluency is CAHAYA's strongest theoretical foundation.** CAHAYA can enforce properties that maximize fluency:
- Consistent alignment (reduces processing effort)
- Clear visual hierarchy (directs attention efficiently)
- Adequate contrast (enables rapid figure-ground separation)
- Symmetry and balance (reduces information load)
- Gestalt grouping (proximity, similarity, continuity)

The key insight: enforce fluency-maximizing properties while ensuring the source is NOT obvious (avoid trivial simplicity).

## PART IV: CURVATURE PREFERENCE — ROBUST FINDING

### 4.1 Bar & Neta (2006, 2007)

**Finding:** Humans prefer curved visual objects over sharp-angled ones. Curved objects were liked 67.2% of the time vs. 50.6% for sharp-angled objects.

**Mechanism:** Sharp transitions in contour convey a sense of threat. fMRI showed significantly greater bilateral amygdala activation for sharp-angled objects compared to curved objects. This response is driven by contour type, not by the liking response itself.

**Key details:**
- Effect observed at just 84ms exposure (pre-conscious)
- Driven by low spatial frequencies (rapid threat detection)
- Amygdala response is function of contour type, not dependent on explicit liking

### 4.2 Replication and Robustness

This is one of the most robustly replicated findings in empirical aesthetics:

- **Cross-cultural:** Gómez-Puerto, Munar, Acedo & Gomila (2013) replicated the effect in Spain and Ghana
- **Developmental:** Quinn, Brown & Streppa (1997) showed 3-4 month old infants have spontaneous preference for curvature
- **Cross-domain:** Effect found in abstract shapes, everyday objects, product design (Westerman et al., 2012), car interiors (Leder & Carbon, 2005), furniture (Dazkir & Read, 2012), architecture (Vartanian et al., 2013, 2019)
- **Cross-species:** Munar et al. (2015) found curvature preference in non-human species

### 4.3 Caveats

- Silvia & Barona (2009) found typicality and symmetry may confound some studies
- Bertamini et al. (2016) asked: "Do observers like curvature or dislike angularity?" — suggests it's avoidance of sharp angles rather than preference for curves
- Leder, Tinio & Bar (2011): Emotional valence has precedence — curved snakes are not preferred over angular teddy bears
- Effect is modulated by context, expertise, and emotional content

### 4.4 Implication for CAHAYA

**CAHAYA can enforce curvature preference as a default UI principle.** This is one of the most scientifically validated aesthetic findings:
- Prefer rounded corners (border-radius) over sharp corners as default
- Warn on excessive sharp angles in UI elements
- Allow designer override for specific intent (warning icons, alert states)
- Curvature detection is 100% computable (contour analysis)

## PART V: STATISTICAL IMAGE PROPERTIES (SIPs)

### 5.1 Redies and Colleagues (2007–2023) — The Jena School

Christoph Redies and collaborators at Friedrich Schiller University Jena have conducted the most systematic program of statistical image property analysis in aesthetics.

**Key Discovery:** Art images and natural scenes share specific statistical properties in their Fourier power spectra — specifically, 1/f² characteristics (power falls as 1/f² with increasing spatial frequency).

### 5.2 Specific Findings

**1/f² Power Spectrum (Koch, Denzler & Redies, 2010):**
- Art images, cartoons, comics, and mangas all possess relatively uniform 1/f² characteristics
- These man-made stimuli form a subset of all images sharing specific Fourier power spectral properties
- Natural scenes also exhibit 1/f² characteristics
- Fourier slope of artworks: typically between -3 and -2 (median: -2.64)

**Self-Similarity (PHOG Analysis):**
- Aesthetic artworks are characterized by: high self-similarity, moderate complexity, and low anisotropy
- In this combination, artworks differ significantly from photographs of natural/man-made scenes

**Edge-Orientation Entropy (Grebenkina et al., 2018):**
- Edge-orientation entropy predicts preference for diverse types of man-made images

**Neural Style Transfer Study (Geller et al., 2022):**
- Statistical image properties of style-transferred images explain 50–69% of variance in aesthetic ratings
- Images rated more Pleasing and Interesting when closer to a "sweet spot" where traditional Western paintings cluster in SIP space

### 5.3 Critical Limitations

**Koch et al. (2010) themselves state:** "1/f² characteristics are neither necessary nor sufficient to induce aesthetic perception." Images with 1/f² characteristics that are not aesthetic can be produced artificially. The property cannot be interpreted as an exclusive feature of aesthetic images.

**Significant overlap** exists between image categories in their eigenspace — the statistical models do not achieve complete separation between aesthetic and non-aesthetic images.

### 5.4 Implication for CAHAYA

**SIPs provide measurable, computable features that correlate with aesthetic quality but do not determine it.** CAHAYA can:
- Compute Fourier spectral slope of UI renderings
- Flag UIs whose spectral properties fall far outside the "art/natural scene" range
- Use edge-orientation entropy as a balance metric
- Self-similarity analysis at multiple scales for consistency checking
- These are WARNING metrics, not hard constraints

## PART VI: COLOR PREFERENCE — ECOLOGICAL VALENCE THEORY

### 6.1 Palmer & Schloss (2010)

"An Ecological Valence Theory of Human Color Preference"

**Core Theory:** People like colors to the degree that they like objects associated with those colors. Color preference arises from average affective responses to color-associated objects.

**Key Evidence:**
- Blues preferred (associated with clear sky, clean water)
- Browns disliked (associated with feces, rotten food)
- WAVE (Weighted Affective Valence Estimate) explains 80% of variance in color preference across colors (r = 0.83)

### 6.2 Robustness

- Replicated by Taylor & Franklin (2012) with British observers (61% variance explained)
- Al-Rasheed (2023) tested with Arabic-speaking population
- EVT outperforms cone-contrast models (Hurlbert & Ling, 2007) and color-appearance models

### 6.3 Critical Limitations

- At the INDIVIDUAL level, EVT performs substantially less well than at the group level
- Sex differences exist: WAVE is a better fit for male preferences (74%) than female (45%)
- Cultural variation in color-object associations can shift preferences
- Context dramatically affects color preference (same color in different contexts evokes different responses)

### 6.4 Implication for CAHAYA

**Color preferences are ecologically grounded and partially predictable, but heavily context-dependent.** CAHAYA can:
- Enforce color contrast ratios (WCAG compliance — 100% computable)
- Warn on high-negative-valence color combinations (browns, murky yellows in non-contextual use)
- Enforce color harmony rules based on color wheel relationships
- Allow theme customization (individual preferences vary legitimately)
- **Cannot** enforce "universally preferred" colors — they don't exist independent of context

## PART VII: NEUROAESTHETICS — THE DEFAULT MODE NETWORK

### 7.1 Vessel, Starr & Rubin (2012, 2013)

"The Brain on Art" and "Art Reaches Within"

**Paradigm-Shifting Discovery:** Intense aesthetic experience activates the Default Mode Network (DMN) — brain regions normally associated with self-referential thought and rest.

### 7.2 Key Findings

**Two distinct neural patterns:**

1. **Sensory-Emotional (Linear):** Activity in occipitotemporal cortex and striatum increased linearly with aesthetic rating. This represents basic perceptual-reward processing.

2. **Self-Relevance (Step Function):** Activity in medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) and other DMN regions showed a STEP-LIKE increase ONLY for the highest-rated artworks ("4" on 4-point scale). Non-differential for all other ratings. This is the signature of deeply moving aesthetic experience.

**Critically Individual:**
- Average pairwise correlation between observers' ratings: 0.13 (extremely low agreement)
- Each image highly rated by one observer was given low ratings by others
- Aesthetic experience is profoundly individual
- Since every image was both loved and disliked by different observers, neural effects reflect the aesthetic EXPERIENCE, not features of the images

### 7.3 The Self-Relevance Interpretation

The DMN activation suggests that certain artworks, though unfamiliar, may be so well-matched to an individual's unique makeup that they obtain access to neural substrates concerned with the SELF — access that external stimuli normally do not get. This mediates a sense of being "moved" or "touched from within."

This is consistent with the modern notion that individuals' taste in art is linked with their sense of IDENTITY.

### 7.4 Vessel et al. (2019) — Generalization

"The Default-Mode Network Represents Aesthetic Appeal That Generalizes Across Visual Domains"

The DMN aesthetic response generalizes across visual domains — not specific to art, but a fundamental neural signature of being aesthetically moved by ANY visual stimulus.

### 7.5 Implication for CAHAYA

**This is the most devastating finding for universal aesthetic enforcement.** The deepest aesthetic experience is:
- Profoundly individual (0.13 correlation between observers)
- Linked to self-relevance and identity
- A step-function phenomenon (not gradual — you're either moved or you're not)
- Cannot be predicted from stimulus features alone

**CAHAYA CANNOT engineer the "moved" experience.** It can only optimize the lower layers (perceptual fluency, contrast, balance) that form the foundation on which individual aesthetic experience builds.

## PART VIII: INFORMATION-THEORETIC APPROACHES

### 8.1 Birkhoff's Aesthetic Measure (1933) — Revisited

M = O/C (aesthetic measure = order / complexity)

**Rigau, Feixas & Sbert (2007)** reinterpreted using information theory:
- Order = algorithmic reduction of uncertainty (H − K)
- Complexity = initial uncertainty (Shannon entropy H)
- M = (H − K) / H = redundancy / entropy

**Application to Art:**
- Mondrian paintings: High order (high compressibility)
- Pollock paintings: Low order (low compressibility)
- Van Gogh: Intermediate

**Limitation:** Complexity ≠ beauty. Can measure one dimension but not full aesthetic experience. Some people prefer high-complexity (Pollock) and others prefer high-order (Mondrian) — individual differences again.

### 8.2 Schmidhuber's Compression Progress Theory (2007, 2009)

**Key Insight:** Beauty = first derivative of subjective compressibility. "Interestingness" = rate of compression improvement.

This is NOT an objective ideal ratio (unlike Birkhoff). It depends on:
- Observer's prior knowledge
- Observer's learning algorithm
- Observer's current state

**Beauty is NOT in the object itself but in the LEARNING PROGRESS of the observer.**

This explains:
- Why the same artwork can be boring (already compressed/understood) or fascinating (compression progress possible) depending on viewer's experience
- Why novelty eventually becomes boring (no more compression progress)
- Why too-complex stimuli are overwhelming (no compression progress achievable)
- INDIVIDUAL DIFFERENCES: Same UI can be beautiful or boring depending on user's familiarity

### 8.3 Kolmogorov Complexity — Compression Ensembles

**Karjus et al. (2023):**
- 112 image transformations + compression
- Analyzed 125,000 artworks
- Multidimensional complexity measure
- Aligns well with human complexity judgments
- Successfully classifies artistic styles, authorship

### 8.4 Implication for CAHAYA

**Information theory provides CAHAYA's most powerful computable metrics:**

- **Shannon Entropy:** H = −Σ p(i) × log₂(p(i)) — measures information distribution across visual layout. CAHAYA can enforce optimal entropy ranges (not too uniform, not too chaotic)
- **Kolmogorov Complexity (approximated via compression):** Measure visual complexity objectively. Flag UIs outside acceptable complexity bounds.
- **Compression Progress:** Cannot be computed universally (observer-dependent), but CAHAYA can estimate first-time-viewer compression progress based on layout structure
- **Birkhoff Ratio (Information-Theoretic):** Useful as one metric among many, not as sole aesthetic criterion

## PART IX: PROCESSING MODELS — LEDER & PREDICTIVE CODING

### 9.1 Leder, Belke, Oeberst & Augustin (2004)

"A Model of Aesthetic Appreciation and Aesthetic Judgments" — Five Processing Stages:

1. **Perceptual Analysis:** Low-level features (color, contrast, symmetry)
2. **Explicit Classification:** Content, style recognition
3. **Implicit Classification:** Familiarity, prototypicality
4. **Cognitive Mastering:** Interpretation, meaning-making
5. **Evaluation:** Cognitive state assessment

**Two Outputs:**
- **Aesthetic Emotion:** Immediate affective response (pleasure)
- **Aesthetic Judgment:** Reflective evaluation

**Key Innovation:** Feedback loops between stages. Aesthetic experience = extended temporal process with multiple cycles. Updated in 2014 review. Widely cited (1000+ citations).

### 9.2 Van de Cruys & Wagemans (2011)

"Putting Reward in Art: A Tentative Prediction Error Account of Visual Art"

**Core Principle:** Brain constantly predicts sensory input. Prediction errors (mismatches) drive aesthetic experience.

**Aesthetic Mechanism:**
1. Artist/designer builds up predictions in viewer
2. Artist/designer violates predictions (creates prediction error)
3. Viewer resolves prediction error (finds pattern on different level)
4. Transition from uncertainty → predictability = REWARD (aesthetic pleasure)

**Why Great Art Violates "Good Gestalt":** Traditional view says beauty = harmony, simplicity. But great artworks VIOLATE these. Predictive coding explains: violations create engaging prediction errors that reward resolution.

### 9.3 Implication for CAHAYA

**Leder's model suggests aesthetic experience is MULTI-STAGE and TEMPORAL:**
- CAHAYA can optimize Stage 1 (perceptual features) — 100% enforceable
- Stage 2-3 (classification, familiarity) — partially enforceable via design system consistency
- Stage 4-5 (meaning, evaluation) — NOT enforceable, requires human designer

**Predictive coding suggests CAHAYA should NOT enforce total regularity.** Perfect homogeneity = no prediction errors = boredom. Some controlled variation within a system is aesthetically positive. CAHAYA can:
- Enforce base consistency (predictable grid, spacing, hierarchy)
- Allow and measure controlled variation (emphasis, surprise elements)
- Warn on both excessive regularity AND excessive irregularity

## PART X: COMPUTATIONAL AESTHETICS — MACHINE LEARNING

### 10.1 NIMA: Neural Image Assessment (Talebi & Milanfar, 2017/2018)

Google's NIMA uses CNNs to predict both aesthetic and technical quality of images. Fine-tuned from ImageNet pre-trained networks using Earth Mover's Loss (EML).

**AVA Dataset:** Over 250,000 images with aesthetic ratings on 1-10 scale from multiple human raters.

### 10.2 State of the Art (2023–2024)

- **MUSIQ (2021):** Multi-scale Image Quality Transformer — handles arbitrary resolutions
- **Transformer-CNN Hybrids:** Combine local (CNN) and global (Transformer) aesthetic features
- **Composition-aware models (ReLIC):** Graph-based representation of spatial relationships for aesthetic prediction

### 10.3 What ML Achieves

- SRCC (Spearman Rank Correlation) with human ratings: 0.65–0.75 on diverse datasets
- Better performance on technical quality than aesthetic quality
- Composition features identified as "most important" aesthetic attribute
- Deep features for aesthetic classification are LARGELY DIFFERENT from those for object classification

### 10.4 Critical Limitations

- Models learn AVERAGE human preferences — cannot predict individual taste
- Black-box nature: cannot explain WHY an image is aesthetically rated high/low
- Cultural bias: trained primarily on Western photographic aesthetics
- Domain-specific: models trained on photos perform poorly on paintings, UI, etc.
- Fusion of hand-crafted aesthetic features with deep CNN features improves accuracy by only ~1.5%

### 10.5 Implication for CAHAYA

**ML aesthetic assessment can serve as an ADVISORY metric, not a compile-time guarantee:**
- Pre-trained models could score UI renders as a post-compilation warning
- Cannot replace formal verification (black-box, non-provable)
- Useful for catching gross aesthetic violations
- CAHAYA's formal approach (compile-time verification) is fundamentally different from ML prediction — CAHAYA proves properties, ML estimates them

### 12.3 The McManus Principle

Named after McManus et al. (2010), this is CAHAYA's governing aesthetic philosophy:

> **Population preferences are WEAK. Individual preferences are STRONG, STABLE, and VARIED.**

Therefore CAHAYA:
1. Enforces what is universally BAD (violations, broken perceptual principles)
2. Optimizes for perceptual FLUENCY (benefits everyone regardless of taste)
3. Provides RANGES rather than POINTS for aesthetic parameters
4. Allows INDIVIDUAL CUSTOMIZATION within validated bounds
5. Reports METRICS without prescribing BEAUTY

> **Beauty is NOT in the object. Beauty is in the COMPRESSION PROGRESS of the observer.**

## APPENDIX A: THE HONEST ASSESSMENT

### What Previous Estimate Got Wrong

The initial estimate of "~95% of beauty mathematically enforceable" was based on:
1. Taking Birkhoff's M=O/C at face value (empirically weak)
2. Assuming the golden ratio has scientific support (it doesn't)
3. Assuming Berlyne's inverted-U provides a universal optimum (it doesn't)
4. Assuming Ramachandran's 8 laws are all equally well-supported (they're not)
5. Ignoring the McManus individual difference finding (devastating for universal enforcement)
6. Ignoring the Vessel DMN finding (aesthetic experience is profoundly individual)

### What the Revised Estimate Is Based On

The revised estimate of "~60-70% enforceable" is based on:
- Layer 1 (Pixel Correctness): 100% — universally computable and verifiable
- Layer 2 (UX Science): 100% — based on robust psychophysical findings
- Layer 3 (Perceptual Principles): 90% — Gestalt, symmetry, alignment all robust
- Layer 4 (Information Theory): 100% — mathematically formal and computable
- Layer 5 (Neuroaesthetic Principles): 70% — curvature strong, others moderate
- Layer 6 (Processing Dynamics): 50% — fluency strong, prediction error individual-dependent
- Layer 7 (Individual/Cultural): 0% — fundamentally human territory

**Weighted across layers (by aesthetic importance):** ~60-70%

### What This Means for CAHAYA's Value Proposition

CAHAYA's guarantee is NOT "if it compiles, it's beautiful." 

CAHAYA's guarantee IS: **"If it compiles, it's perceptually sound, informationally optimized, and free of known aesthetic violations."**

This is still enormously valuable. Most bad UI is bad because it violates Layers 1-4, which CAHAYA can completely enforce. The remaining 30-40% (individual taste, cultural resonance, emotional impact) is what human designers do. CAHAYA removes the mechanical foundation so designers can focus on the creative peak.
